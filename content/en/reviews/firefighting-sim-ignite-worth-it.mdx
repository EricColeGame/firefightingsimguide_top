## Core Gameplay Mechanics and Dynamic Fire Physics

The foundational pillar of any dedicated emergency services title is how convincingly it portrays hazard propagation. Rather than treating flames as static environmental textures with hit points, this simulation models combustion through dynamic heat buildup, ventilation pathways, and fuel consumption. Fire spreads across distinct materials at varied velocities, meaning a small kitchen blaze can rapidly develop into a rolling structural conflagration if you fail to isolate oxygen supplies or miscalculate your attack vector.

Ventilation tactics play a critical operational role. Shattering a window without first staging a charged attack line introduces fresh oxygen, triggering dangerous flashovers or sudden backdrafts that can incapacitate your team. Breaching doors requires tactical assessment: checking door temperature with a thermal imaging camera, establishing a perimeter, and coordinating ventilation cuts on rooftops. This layered hazard model elevates the title beyond standard arcade hose-and-run experiences into a genuine operational puzzle.

Laying supply lines from municipal hydrants to pump panels adds genuine logistics to every callout, forcing players to manage hose kinks, water pressure limits, and structural obstructions.

## Actionable Tips for New Incident Commanders

Before donning your turnout coat and hitting the fireground, implementing sound tactical habits will keep your squad safe and your mission scores high:

1. **Perform a 360-Degree Size-Up**: Never charge straight through the front door. Circle the exterior to identify secondary egress points, locate the seat of the fire through windows, and identify trapped victims.
2. **Control the Flow Path**: Keep exterior doors closed until your attack line is charged and ready. Leaving doors wide open feeds oxygen directly to the fire seat.
3. **Scan with the Thermal Camera**: Use your thermal imaging camera systematically from floor to ceiling. Identifying heat signatures behind walls prevents surprise flashovers above your escape route.
4. **Coordinate Hydraulic Ventilation**: After knocking down main interior flames, set your fog nozzle near an exterior window pointed outside to rapidly siphon trapped smoke out of the structure.
5. **Protect Your Supply Line**: Always designate an initial team member to connect the supply line to a hydrant immediately upon arrival to prevent tank water depletion mid-attack.

Applying these real-world tactical fundamentals elevates your effectiveness and turns difficult five-alarm emergency calls into clean, controlled victories.
